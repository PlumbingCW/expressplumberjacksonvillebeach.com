How to Find an Underground Water Leak at Your Home

The quickest check is the meter test. Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, then watch the meter's small leak indicator or the dial. If it keeps moving, water is escaping somewhere, and if the inside of the house is dry, the leak is likely in the line between the meter and the house.

After that, walk the yard looking for soft ground, a green strip of grass or a damp patch. A homeowner can confirm a leak this way. A plumber with listening gear and a pressure gauge pins down where it is.

The meter test, step by step

Do this before you dig or call

  • Shut off all water use inside and outside. That includes the ice maker, the washer, the dishwasher and any irrigation timer.
  • Open the meter box lid. Wear gloves and check for insects or debris first.
  • Find the leak indicator, usually a small triangle or a wheel that spins when water flows. A moving indicator means water is flowing somewhere.
  • Write down the register reading, wait about half an hour without using water, then read it again.
  • If the reading changed, you have a leak. If it did not, the leak is not constant or the problem is elsewhere.

Next, close the main shutoff valve at the house and read the meter again. If the indicator stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is outside the house on the supply line.

A toilet that runs on silently is the most common false alarm. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color shows up in the bowl without flushing, you have found a leak and it is not underground.

A single sign is a hint. Two or three together, especially a spinning meter with a dry house, make the case. Document what you see with photos and dates, because the same notes help you talk to your insurer or to the city.

What a plumber uses to find the exact spot

Digging blind costs time and damages the yard. Professionals use tools that find the leak first, so the excavation is small and aimed.

Acoustic listening

A sensitive microphone and ground sensor pick up the hiss of water escaping under pressure. We listen at the meter, the valves and along the pipe route.

Pressure testing

We isolate sections of line, gauge the pressure and watch for a drop. This tells us which section has the leak before anyone digs.

Thermal imaging

A thermal camera shows temperature differences that hot water lines create when they leak into a slab or wall, which helps with hot-side problems.

Tracer gas

A safe, non-toxic gas mixture is pushed into the line and a detector finds where it escapes. It works when sound-based tools are confused by noise.

The right tool depends on the pipe material, the depth and the noise around the site. A good technician often combines two methods to confirm the location. Our leak detection service uses that approach and gives a clear price before any digging starts.

What you can do while you wait

If the meter test shows a leak, shut off the main valve at the house if you can reach it, then keep the water off until a plumber has looked. A small leak can undermine a driveway, a walkway or a foundation if left running.

Do not start digging along the pipe route. You can hit gas, electric or irrigation lines, and you may crack the pipe worse. Mark the damp area with flags or stakes and photograph it instead.

Who owns the pipe and who fixes it

The line from the meter to the house is usually the homeowner's responsibility, while the water main on the street side and the meter itself belong to the water utility. That split decides whether you call the city or a plumber.

If the leak is on the private side, a licensed plumber can repair or replace the section. Our water line repair work covers spot repairs and full replacements and starts with a clear price before work starts.

Where the city's side ends in Jacksonville Beach

The City of Jacksonville Beach provides its own water and sewer. Problems on the city side of the meter, such as a main break, go to the city. The water and sewer office is at 904-247-6241 from 8:00 to 4:30 on weekdays, and the emergency line listed for water and sewer is 904-247-6278. If water is bubbling up at the street or around the meter itself, call the city first, because the repair is theirs.

Problems inside the property line go to a plumber. Jacksonville Beach is at or near sea level and about two-thirds of the municipal area is water, so the ground can stay damp for reasons that have nothing to do with a leak, and wet spots can mislead. That is a good reason to trust the meter test over what the lawn looks like.

When a small leak is still urgent

A slow leak under a driveway can cost more than a burst pipe over time because it runs unnoticed and washes out soil. A sudden loss of pressure, a loud hiss or water pooling near the foundation should be treated as urgent.

Insurance usually covers sudden and accidental damage, not wear, gradual leaks or the failed part itself. Check your own policy, photograph everything and keep your water bills, because they show when the leak started.

Repairs to buried water lines follow the IPC based Florida Building Code, Plumbing, so ask the city building division whether your repair needs a permit before anyone digs.
