The permit comes before the plumber touches the tank

How the city permit form shapes an install

Jacksonville Beach plumbing work is filed through the Building Inspection Division's plumbing e-permit. The application asks you to classify the job as alteration, new, re-pipe, repair, fuel gas or other. It also asks whether your water source is city or well, and whether your gas is natural or propane. The application states that work may not begin until the permit is approved and posted on site.

That wording has practical consequences. A new heater in a new location is not the same as a like-for-like swap, and gas work carries its own classification. We pick the classification that fits your job and keep the posted permit visible on site. The city follows the 2023 edition of the Florida Building Code for plans. Owners doing their own work sign different paperwork, so ask the Building Inspection Division at 904-247-6100 before you start.

Switching fuel is possible but adds work: a new gas run, a new circuit or a different vent. We tell you before the permit goes in whether the switch is sensible for your house.

Where the heater goes: garage, closet and venting

Location matters as much as the model. Many local homes are on slabs with the heater in the garage, and a water heater in a garage or closet needs combustion air if it burns gas, and a clear working space for the person who services it later. A heater tucked behind shelving or squeezed under a stair is difficult to flush or repair.

Venting for gas heaters has to carry exhaust out of the building on a continuous route. We check the existing flue and replace it when it is corroded or undersized. For a hybrid unit, we look for open space, because heat pump heaters cool the air around them and need to draw air in.

If you are considering an on-demand unit, the venting and gas supply change more than they do for a tank. The tankless water heater page covers service, and we will review the gas load and vent route on a site visit.

Expansion tanks, relief valves and the small parts that decide how long it lasts

Parts we check or add

  • Expansion tank, when the home has a closed system that can trap pressure from heated water.
  • Temperature and pressure relief valve, with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot.
  • Drain pan and drain line where a leak could damage a floor or wall.
  • Shutoff valve on the cold supply and flexible connectors that suit the pipe material.
  • Dielectric connections where dissimilar metals meet, to slow corrosion.
  • A service shutoff and a clean, level surface for the unit.

Hot water that arrives too hot, or relief valves that weep after every heating cycle, are usually signs that an expansion tank is missing or failed. If you already have a leaking or tired heater, see water heater repair before you commit to a new install, and water heater replacement if the tank has failed.

New installs are done to Florida's plumbing code, which takes its model language from the IPC. Gas units also follow the Florida Building Code, Fuel Gas, and your city building division can tell you whether a permit and inspection apply to your address.
