The permit comes before the pipe

In Jacksonville Beach, plumbing work starts with a permit. The city uses a plumbing e-permit managed by the Building Inspection Division, and its application states that work may not begin until a permit is approved and posted on site. If you are planning a remodel, build the permit step into the schedule, not the end.

The application asks for the classification of work: alteration, new, re-pipe, repair, fuel gas or other. It also asks whether your water source is city or well and whether gas is natural or propane. Pick the wrong box and the paperwork can stall, so we fill it out for jobs we install.

Work types listed on the city plumbing application

  • DWV on private property (drain, waste and vent piping)
  • Gas piping only
  • Grease interceptor
  • Irrigation
  • Pool piping

City plans follow the Florida Building Code 2023 edition. If you plan to do some of the work yourself, the city has different signature requirements for owners, including an Owner Builder Disclosure Statement. For anything about which jobs need a permit, ask the Building Inspection Division at 904-247-6100.

Supply and backflow connections for irrigation, plus pool piping, both of which are listed permit types on the city form.

Rough-in for a new bathroom or remodel

Rough-in is the plumbing inside the walls and floor before the finish surfaces go on. Drains must slope correctly, vents have to connect to the stack, and supply lines need the right fittings at the right heights. Once the tile and drywall are in, nothing here can be fixed without demolition.

We set the drain, vent and supply stubs to match your fixture specifications. Before covering anything, we pressure test the supply lines and fill the drains with water to check for leaks. The city inspection happens at this stage too, so the inspector can see the work before it disappears. If an older house needs its supply lines replaced as part of the project, that is covered under repiping.

Check the fixtures before the walls close

Have the faucet, shower valve and toilet model chosen before rough-in. Different fixtures need different supply heights and rough-in dimensions, and a change after the walls are closed costs time. If you have not picked yet, tell us and we will walk through the choices.

Kitchen plumbing installation

A kitchen remodel touches several lines at once: the sink supply, the drain with its vent, a dishwasher hookup, a disposal and sometimes a gas line to a new range. We lay out all of these together so the drain trap is not fighting the cabinet and the stop valves are reachable.

If you want a new disposal, we install the unit and the drain connection together. For problems with an existing unit, our garbage disposal repair team can fix or replace it. We also make sure the dishwasher drain loops correctly so wastewater cannot siphon back into the sink.

New construction plumbing

On a new build, plumbing happens in stages. Under-slab drains go in before the concrete is poured, then the rough-in goes into the framing, and the finish fixtures arrive at the end. We coordinate with your builder so each stage is ready when the other trades need it.

The building inspection steps line up with those stages. The city can only inspect what it can see, so the order matters: test before cover, every time.

New plumbing is built to the code standard for Florida plumbing, the IPC based Florida Building Code, Plumbing, with the permit and inspection steps set by the city building division. Inspection happens while the lines are still open, which is why the walls stay open until it passes.
