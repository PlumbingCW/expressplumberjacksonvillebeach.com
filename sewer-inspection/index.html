Before you buy: a camera look at the sewer lateral

A home inspection looks at fixtures and visible piping, but it does not see underground. The sewer lateral is the pipe from the house to the city connection, and it is yours to maintain once you own the property. A camera pass before closing shows whether you are inheriting roots, a crushed section or a pipe that is near the end of its life.

Findings give you room to negotiate. You can ask the seller to repair, negotiate the price, or walk away informed. Ask for the video and a written note of where each defect sits, measured from the cleanout, so any later contractor can find it.

What the camera actually shows

Cracks and offsets

Joints that have shifted or pipe that has split along its length appear as dark gaps or steps in the wall.

Roots

Fine white hairs or thick tangles at the joints, which are signs of entry points and moisture escaping.

Bellies and sags

A low spot holds water. On video you see a standing pool and a pipe that appears to dip.

Scale and grease

A rough or coated wall that shrinks the bore, common in older cast iron and kitchen lines.

Collapse or missing pipe

A crushed section or an open void where soil shows through.

Reading the video with the plumber

The footage looks confusing until someone explains it. The plumber stops at each defect, names it, and tells you the distance from the access point. A locator at ground level marks the spot on the surface, so a repair crew can dig in the right place and not a trench away.

The recording is the evidence you keep if you later need a repair estimate or a second opinion.

If the camera finds damage: permit and repair form

How a repair permit works in Jacksonville Beach

A camera finding is not a repair order. If the pipe needs work, the City of Jacksonville Beach Building Inspection Division handles plumbing e-permits. The application asks for a classification such as alteration, new, re-pipe or repair, and a type such as drain, waste and vent on private property. It also states that work may not begin until the permit is approved and posted on site.

That affects timing. If you are mid-sale, a repair plan with permit steps is easier to explain to a buyer than an open question. Ask the Building Inspection Division at 904-247-6100 whether your specific repair needs a permit, and we will schedule around the answer. For the repair itself, see sewer line repair.

Using a camera to solve repeat drain trouble

If the same drain keeps backing up, a cable run is only a bandage. The camera tells you what the cable could not: a root mass at a joint, a belly that collects solids, or a clean pipe that points to a different cause. The plumber then picks a fix that matches the finding.

The video also helps decide how to clean. Roots and grease may justify hydro jetting, while a cracked pipe may not tolerate pressure. A camera pass first prevents the wrong tool from making things worse. Sewer line cleaning follows if the line only needs to be cleared.

What to do before the plumber comes

Prepare for the inspection

  • Locate your cleanout and clear the area around it.
  • Note which fixtures have been slow or backing up, and when.
  • Tell us if the house is part of a sale, so we can give you clear video and notes.
  • Avoid running a lot of water just before we arrive, so the line is not full on the first pass.
