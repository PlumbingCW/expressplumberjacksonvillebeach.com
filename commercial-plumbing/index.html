Kitchens and dining rooms near the beach

Beach-area restaurants run hard when visitors arrive, and the plumbing carries the load: pre-rinse sinks, floor drains, dish machines, prep sinks and grease lines all working through a busy service. A backup during dinner costs more than the repair itself. We prioritize restaurant calls and aim to book a same-day visit when the schedule allows.

Floor drains that gurgle, sinks that drain slowly after the lunch rush, and a sewer odor in the dining room are early warnings. They usually point to grease and food buildup in the line, and clearing it with a cable or hydro jetting before it closes completely is far easier than a dinner-hour shutdown.

Grease interceptors and the city's FOG program

The City of Jacksonville Beach runs a Grease Interceptor program, commonly called FOG for fats, oils and grease, under Ordinances 2019-8125 and 2020-8156. The city also offers a rebate program tied to it. If you operate a food business, the practical consequence is that your interceptor needs to be kept in working order and serviced, and the program may affect what you install or repair.

Ask the Building Inspection Division at 904-247-6100 what applies to your location. Grease interceptor work is one of the project types on the city's plumbing e-permit, and work may not begin until the permit is approved and posted. Planning ahead avoids a stalled kitchen build.

Grease trap cleaning before it becomes an emergency

A grease trap works by slowing wastewater so grease floats to the top and solids sink. Once the trap is full, grease passes through and builds up in the line. Regular pumping and cleaning keeps it working, and the right interval depends on your volume and menu. Ask us to inspect yours and we will suggest a schedule.

Burst lines and active leaks in occupied buildings

A burst line in a commercial space can run through ceilings, stock and equipment. Close the nearest valve, then the main if needed, and call. We repair the break, and we can find hidden leaks with our leak detection methods, which are modest in a commercial setting but useful when a utility bill climbs with no visible cause.

For multi-tenant buildings, we identify which unit the line serves and which valves isolate it, so one tenant's problem does not shut down everyone.

What to do before we arrive at a business

Safe steps while you wait

  • Shut off the water at the main valve if water is spreading, and do not wait for a manager to approve it
  • Switch off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water
  • Move stock, paper and electronics away from the leak and photograph the damage for your insurer
  • Keep customers and staff out of any area with sewage on the floor and call us on the 24/7 line

Commercial work is held to the state plumbing code, Florida Building Code, Plumbing, a code that follows the IPC and to the fire and health rules that apply to the building's use. Grease interceptors and backflow devices are a city and utility matter, so we confirm those requirements before quoting.

Backflow testing and storm drains

A backflow preventer keeps dirty water from flowing backward into the building supply, and it is typically tested on a schedule. Ask your water utility what is required for your assembly and how often. We can test, repair and replace the device and document the result.

Storm drains and lot drainage get plugged with leaves, trash and sand, and a clogged one leaves parking areas under water in a heavy rain. We clear them and inspect for damage. Keep in mind that a problem in the street's sewer or water main belongs to the city, and our sewer line repair team handles private lines inside your property.

Landlords, property managers and owners

If you manage rentals, you need quick triage and clear paperwork. We document findings with photos and notes so you can bill tenants or file claims, and we communicate with the person on site. You decide the scope, and we give a clear price before work starts.
