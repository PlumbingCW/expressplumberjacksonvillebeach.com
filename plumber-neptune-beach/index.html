Neptune Beach emergency plumbing, from the town next door

Neptune Beach has its own city hall, its own water and sewer, and its own history. It was incorporated on August 11, 1931 after leaving Jacksonville Beach in a tax dispute, and the name comes from a train station called Neptune that Dan G. Wheeler built in 1922. For a plumbing emergency, the line between the two towns means nothing. A pipe breaks the same way on either side.

Emergency dispatch available around the clock means a licensed plumber can be sent at night, on a weekend or on a holiday, and we take the call whenever it comes. We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic and the job in front of yours change it. We do promise a clear price before work starts.

About 7,217 people lived in Neptune Beach at the 2020 census, in roughly 3,500 housing units, and 66 percent of the city's area is water. That matters for plumbing: with water so close and the land so low, drains and buried lines sit in wet ground, and a small defect can become a backup or a leak quickly.

Drain and sewer backups come first

The most common emergency in this part of the beaches is a drain that stops or a sewer line that backs up into a tub or floor drain. Slow drains in more than one fixture, gurgling toilets and water standing in a shower point to the main line, not a single trap.

We clear the line with a cable or, for roots and grease, with water-jet equipment. If one fixture is stopped, emergency drain cleaning usually fixes it. If more than one is backing up, a sewer line cleaning visit is the next step, and a camera shows us whether the problem is a clog or a damaged pipe.

Neptune Beach runs its own sewer. If the backup is in the street or the city main, the city handles it, and City Hall can be reached at 904-270-2400. If the clog is inside your property line, it is a plumber's job. We can help you figure out which one you have.

Which side of the line is the problem?

Water backing up in your lowest drain with a clear cleanout outside usually means the blockage is downstream of the cleanout. If neighbors report the same thing, call City Hall at 904-270-2400. If only your house is affected, call us.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak: Before We Arrive

These steps limit damage while our plumber is on the way. They do not replace a repair, but they make it easier.

1

Shut off the water

Turn the main supply valve clockwise until it stops. In most houses it is near the meter, at the street side or where the line enters the home.

2

Kill power if water is near electrics

If water is near outlets, panels or appliances, turn off the breaker for that area. Stay out of standing water if the floor is wet near power.

3

Open the low faucets

Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the lines, and flush toilets once to empty the tank and bowl.

4

Photograph and clear the area

Take photos for your insurer, then move rugs, furniture and electronics away from the water and put towels or buckets in place.

Leaks, broken pipes and what to do about them

A burst pipe is obvious. A hidden leak is not. We see leaks that show up as a spike in the water bill, a warm spot on a floor, damp drywall or a meter that keeps turning with everything off. Our leak detection crew uses acoustic listening, thermal imaging and pressure tests so we can find the source without tearing out more than we need to.

Where the leak is decides how much gets opened up, so we locate it before we cut or dig. For a cracked supply line that needs attention now, burst pipe repair is the service to ask about, and we shut off and isolate the broken run first.

Water heater failures, day or night

A tank that leaks, a heater that goes cold overnight or a gas heater with a pilot problem can all become emergencies. A leaking tank usually means replacement. If you smell gas, leave the home and call your gas utility or 911 before anything else. For a cold shower with no leak, we diagnose the element, thermostat or gas parts and repair what is worth repairing. See water heater repair for what we check.

Plumbing work in Neptune Beach is built to the IPC based Florida Building Code, Plumbing, the same state code that applies in Jacksonville Beach, and the city's own department decides when a permit is needed.
