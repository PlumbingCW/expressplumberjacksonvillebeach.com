Tank failing right now? Repair or replace, decided in one table

Most people call us with one question: is it worth fixing? The answer depends on where the failure is. A bad part on a sound tank is a repair. A failed tank is not, no matter how new the parts around it are.

Repair or replace: how we decide

Tank, tankless or hybrid heat pump: picking the replacement

A standard tank is the simplest swap and usually the lowest purchase price. It fits the same footprint in most garages and closets and needs no changes to gas or wiring when we match the old unit. Its trade-off is standby heat loss, since it keeps a full tank hot all day.

A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so it saves floor space and does not run out during a long shower. It often needs a larger gas line or a bigger electrical circuit, plus venting changes, which makes it a bigger project. Our tankless water heater page covers service and maintenance once one is installed.

A hybrid heat pump heater pulls heat from the surrounding air and uses far less electricity than a standard electric tank. It needs room around it for airflow and a place to drain condensate. A garage in a warm climate suits it well, a tight closet does not.

Pick a standard tank if

You want the same-day swap, the lowest first cost and the least disruption to existing gas, electric and venting.

Pick tankless if

Space matters, hot water demand is high and the home can support the gas line, circuit and vent a tankless unit needs.

Pick a hybrid if

The heater is electric today, there is open space with airflow, and lower running cost matters more than speed.

Ask us first if

The heater sits in an attic, a tight closet or a spot with no drain, because location can decide the type before preference does.

The city e-permit and what your water does to a new tank

Permit and water quality: two local items to settle before install day

Plumbing work in Jacksonville Beach goes through the Building Inspection Division's plumbing e-permit, and the application states that work may not begin until a permit is approved and posted on site. For you, that means a replacement is not a same-hour swap on paper even when the plumber is ready. We handle the filing and tell you what the city needs. Ask the Building Inspection Division at 904-247-6100 about any special case, such as an owner doing their own work.

The city's water comes from six wells in the Floridan Aquifer, aerated to remove hydrogen sulfide and chlorinated for disinfection. The city's water quality report does not list a hardness figure, so test your tap before assuming scale will be a problem. If you ever noticed a rotten-egg smell from hot water only, that points to the heater's anode rod, not the city supply, and it is something to mention when we pick a replacement.

What a replacement visit actually includes

On the day

  • Shut off and drain the old tank, then disconnect water, gas or power and any venting.
  • Haul away the old unit and check the drain pan, the floor and the surrounding wall for water damage.
  • Set the new heater level, connect new flexible water connectors and a proper shutoff valve.
  • Install a relief valve with a correctly routed discharge pipe, and an expansion tank where the system needs one.
  • Test gas connections for leaks or electrical connections for correct wiring, fill the tank, purge air and check the temperature setting.
  • Show you where the shutoffs and reset controls are and what to watch for in the first week.

If you are planning ahead instead of dealing with a flood, water heater installation covers the scheduled version of this job in more detail.

A replacement tank is installed to the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and a gas unit also falls under the Florida Building Code, Fuel Gas. Ask whether your job needs a permit, because the city building division decides that, not the plumber.
