Jetting or snaking: which one your line needs

A cable punches a hole through a blockage. A hydro jet is a hose with a nozzle that fires water backward and sideways, so it scrubs the whole inside of the pipe and flushes the debris out ahead of it. If your drain opens, then slows again in a month, the cable did its job and left the coating behind.

Roots and repeat clogs: what the jet removes

Tree and shrub roots find the tiny seams at pipe joints and push in for the moisture. Once inside, they grow into a mat that catches paper and wipes. A jet with a cutting nozzle trims root hairs and washes the debris down the line, so water has a clear bore again.

Roots grow back if the joint stays open. We tell you plainly when jetting is a clean-out and when the real fix is a joint repair. A camera pass before and after shows the pipe wall, so you see the evidence and not just our word.

A snake got stuck or broke in the line

A cable that stops and will not advance usually met a hard obstruction: a belly full of settled debris, an offset joint, or a section of root mass thicker than the cable head. Forcing it can kink the cable or push the blockage tighter. Stop and call so we can look before anyone pulls harder.

If the cable is already stuck, do not yank it with a wrench. A plumber can feed a camera alongside the cable in many cases, find what holds it, and decide whether to jet around it or repair the pipe. This is where drain cleaning ends and diagnosis begins.

Jetting does not change a pipe's code requirements, but it shows whether the line meets them. A line cleared by jetting and then camera checked can reveal a sag or offset that the code standard for Florida plumbing, the IPC based Florida Building Code, Plumbing would not allow in new work.

When jetting is the wrong tool: camera first

Old pipe, especially clay, orangeburg and thin cast iron, can already be cracked, offset or partially collapsed. High pressure water in a pipe like that can widen a crack, wash soil into the line, or leave you with a bigger repair. A camera shows what the pipe looks like before we decide how much pressure is safe.

Signs we should skip the jet and look first

  • The cable stops at the same distance every time.
  • Water backs up again within days of any clearing.
  • A low, wet or sunken area appears in the yard over the line.
  • The house is old and the line has never been inspected.

If the camera finds damage, we explain it and move to sewer line repair options. If the pipe is sound, we jet with confidence and record the run.

Restaurants and the city grease interceptor program

Grease interceptors in Jacksonville Beach

The City of Jacksonville Beach runs a Grease Interceptor program under Ordinances 2019-8125 and 2020-8156, with a rebate program attached. Food service businesses are the main audience, since kitchen grease is what builds the hard coating that jetting removes. If you operate a restaurant, ask the city about the program before you sign a maintenance plan.

For homeowners the lesson is the same on a smaller scale. A beach house with a busy kitchen and a long run to the city connection builds grease slowly. When we find that coating, a jet cleans it, and a conversation about what goes down the sink keeps it from rebuilding. For restaurant lines see also commercial plumbing.

What jetting looks like at your house

We access the line through a cleanout, usually outside near the house or inside a utility space. The hose feeds in with the nozzle pulling itself forward while the water works. Debris and water flow down the line, so we place a bucket or a vacuum at the access to catch what comes back.

The job ends with a camera pass when the line is old or the problem was serious. That lets us confirm the bore is open, note any belly or offset, and give you a plain recommendation. Sewer camera work is the same pass offered on its own.
