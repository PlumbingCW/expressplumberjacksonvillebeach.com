What the city report says, and what it leaves out

Six wells, aeration and chlorine: reading the water report

The City of Jacksonville Beach runs its own water system from six wells in the Floridan Aquifer. The water is aerated to remove hydrogen sulfide, the rotten-egg smell, and chlorinated for disinfection. The city's 2025 report lists lead and copper results with zero sites above the action level. It does not state hardness.

That gap decides how you should shop. A softener answers a hardness problem, and without a number you could be buying a solution to a problem you do not have. Test your tap with a hardness kit or a lab, and ask the Water Division at 904-247-6241 for the annual report. Then bring the result to us and we match equipment to it.

No single device does everything. A softener does not remove chlorine, a carbon filter does not remove hardness and RO does not feed a whole house. Most homes need one of these, not all of them, and some need none.

When water smells like rotten eggs in a city-treated home

The city aerates its well water to strip hydrogen sulfide, so a strong sulfur smell on every cold tap is worth reporting to the Water Division. A smell that appears only on the hot side usually belongs to the water heater, where bacteria react with the tank's anode rod. A plumber can flush the tank or swap the rod.

If the smell comes from a drain and not the water, that is a drain or trap problem, and drain cleaning is the fix. We trace which source you have before suggesting treatment equipment.

Repairing a softener or conditioner that stopped working

Softeners fail in predictable ways. A salt bridge forms and the tank runs on empty. The brine line clogs, a control head stops regenerating, or the resin wears out. Filters clog when cartridges are not changed. We diagnose which part has failed before suggesting a new system.

Signs your softener or filter needs service

  • Scale and spots return even though the softener is plugged in.
  • The salt level never drops, which suggests a bridge or a stuck regeneration.
  • Water pressure drops across the house after a filter cartridge ages.
  • The control head shows an error or runs regeneration at odd hours.
  • You taste salt or notice slick water, which can mean a setting problem.
  • Scale keeps coming back at the heater, and a water heater repair visit turns up sediment in the tank.

What installation looks like in a Jacksonville Beach house

A whole-house system goes at the main line after the shutoff, with a bypass so you can isolate it. It needs a drain for regeneration water, a power outlet for the control head and floor space. The garage is the usual spot. Because installs involve plumbing, check with the Building Inspection Division at 904-247-6100 about permit needs. We explain how the job is classified.

Installing a treatment system also pairs well with a planned water heater installation, since both tie into the main supply and a scale-free supply protects the new heater.

When to call instead of adjusting it yourself

Call a plumber if you see

  • Water around the softener base or a drain line that runs when it should be idle
  • A bypass valve that will not move or leaks when turned
  • Hot water that smells or tastes different after a filter change, which can point to the water heater and not the filter
  • Salt or brine in the drain or on the floor, because it corrodes fittings and floor drains over time

A softener or filter connects to your water lines under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the drain from a softener must be an approved air gap or similar protected connection. Ask the city building division whether your install needs a permit.
