The meter is the dividing line: city side and private side

Jacksonville Beach runs its own water system, so the city owns the main in the street and the meter. Everything from the meter to your house is private, and that line is yours to maintain. A geyser at the curb or a broken main in the street is a city problem. A soggy strip of lawn between the meter and the house is a private problem.

If you think the break is on the city side, call the city water and sewer emergency line at 904-247-6278. During weekday office hours, the City water and sewer office at 904-247-6241 can also help. If the leak is on your side, or you cannot tell, call us. We can find the break and tell you plainly which side it is on, which saves you a wasted call to the wrong party.

Water coming up from the ground right now

A bubbling spot in the yard, a wet patch that is spreading, or a meter box full of water is an active leak. Close the shutoff valve on the house side of the meter if you can reach it, and do not touch the city's valve at the meter. If water is rising near the foundation or the electrical equipment, call right away. We do after-hours repairs, and same-day visits are available when the schedule allows.

Low pressure that came on suddenly

A gradual drop in pressure across the whole house often points to a leak or a partially failed section of the service line. Check first whether it is every fixture or only one. If only the hot side is weak, the water heater or its lines are the likelier cause. If both hot and cold are weak and the meter dial is spinning when nothing is running, the buried line is a prime suspect.

We test pressure at an outdoor spigot, then at the meter side of the shutoff, to see where it falls off. Other causes of low pressure include a partly closed valve, a failing pressure regulator, or clogged fixtures. We rule those out before recommending excavation.

Finding an underground leak without digging up the yard

We start with a meter test to confirm that water is leaving the system, then isolate the service line from the house plumbing. If the line holds pressure on its own, the leak is inside. If it loses pressure, the leak is buried. Ground microphones and acoustic listening then mark the loudest spot along the line.

For confirming suspicious hidden leaks around the house first, see our leak detection page. If the damage extends to the home, we also look at the slab area, since a failing line near the foundation can affect the floor.

Spot repair or full replacement of the service line

A single break in a line that is otherwise sound can be repaired in a short trench with a new section and proper couplings. If the line is old, has patched sections, or fails again, replacing the whole run from the meter to the house is more sensible than chasing the next failure.

Trenchless methods exist for some lines, and open trenching is common for most. We tell you which makes sense based on what is above the pipe, such as driveways, walkways, roots and landscaping. Ask the Building Inspection Division at 904-247-6100 whether your repair needs a permit.

Water service lines, fittings and shutoffs are covered by Florida's plumbing code, which takes its model language from the IPC. The meter and the line up to it belong to the utility, so the utility decides who repairs what on that side.
