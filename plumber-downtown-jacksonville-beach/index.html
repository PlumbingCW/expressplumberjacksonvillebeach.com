Where downtown is, and why plumbing differs here

The City of Jacksonville Beach describes its Downtown Redevelopment District as 185 acres from 13th Avenue South to 9th Avenue North, running from Third Street to the ocean. The city pages do not say how much of that land is residential and how much is commercial, so we do not put a number on it. What is clear on the ground is a mix of uses, and a mix changes the plumbing questions.

A restaurant kitchen puts grease into the drain line. A condo building shares risers and stacks across many units. A single house a few blocks in has its own line to the street. Each one has a different first call, and we start by asking which one you have.

Drainage and flooding downtown

Downtown has had a lot of attention for stormwater. City engineer remarks at a council meeting described enlarging the Central Basin, widening three culverts including ones at 9th Avenue South and Fairway, and ordering extra pumps for the central and south pump stations. One described pump capacity was 15,000 gallons a minute. After a heavy October 2026 storm of six to eight inches of rain, the mayor credited stormwater investment, particularly downtown, for avoiding major flooding.

That is public drainage, and it is the city's job. A plumber works on the pipes inside your property line. The two meet at a few points. If water backs up in a floor drain during heavy rain, the cause can be the city system, your own lateral or both, and a camera inspection of your lateral separates them.

Water coming up through a floor drain?

Stop using water, check whether neighbors have the same problem, and call. A line that is clear when the weather is dry but backs up in heavy rain points toward capacity in the system, while a line that backs up every day points toward a blockage on your side.

Restaurants and small businesses

The city has a fats, oils and grease program, and its documents mention a Grease Interceptor Rebate under city ordinances. The page does not list dollar amounts, so ask the city before you plan around one. Grease is a common reason a kitchen line stops, and regular hydro jetting clears it better than a cable alone. Water and sewer service downtown

The City of Jacksonville Beach provides water and sewer service for the city area. City Hall is at 11 N. Third Street, 904-247-6241. If a problem is in the street or the city main, report it there. If it is inside your property line, it is a plumber's job. Electric and gas come from Beaches Energy, a separate utility.
