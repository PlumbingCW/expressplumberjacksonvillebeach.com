These steps limit damage until a plumber arrives, and none of them need tools.

1

Close the main shutoff

It is usually a ball valve or a round gate valve near the water meter or where the line enters the house. Turn it fully closed. If you cannot find or move it, the meter box has a city-side valve, but leave that one to the city.

2

Relieve pressure

Open the lowest faucet in the house and a few others upstairs. Water in the pipes drains out and the leak slows.

3

Kill power in wet zones

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, do not stand in it. Shut breakers only if you can reach the panel dry.

4

Photograph and move things

Take photos of the damage before cleanup, then lift furniture and electronics out of the wet area.

What a burst or cracked pipe looks like

The obvious version is water spraying from a wall or ceiling. The quiet version is a pinhole that leaks under pressure, a hairline crack in a drain line, or a split along a copper pipe that stains the wall below it. A sudden drop in water pressure, a swelling ceiling, or the sound of water with every fixture off are all signs worth acting on.

If the water has been running for a while before you noticed, the extent of the damage may be beyond the visible spot. For hidden damage, our leak detection team can trace the path.

Why pipes fail in coastal homes

Common causes we see

  • Corrosion at joints and along older copper or galvanized steel
  • High water pressure that stresses fittings and supply hoses
  • Ground movement or settling that pulls on buried lines
  • Tree roots pressing on or cracking drain lines
  • Failed fittings, worn compression connections and brittle plastic
  • An occasional hard freeze, which is rare here but can split exposed lines and outdoor piping

A drain pipe that cracks does not spray. It leaks wastewater into walls or ground and often announces itself with odor or a slow stain. We repair those with the same urgency, because wastewater damage is worse than clean water damage.

Storm season and burst pipes

Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30, and Jacksonville Beach has real history to point to. Hurricane Matthew in October 2016 brought a mandatory evacuation and major flooding, and it partly destroyed the pier. If you leave town for a storm, closing the main before you go removes the chance that a failed hose or line pours water into an empty house for days.

When you return after a storm or a long power outage, check for wet floors, listen for running water, and look at the meter before opening the taps. Flooded or shifted ground can damage buried lines even when the house looks fine. If anything looks off, we can come out for a same-day visit or an after-hours emergency.

Emergency repairs are still held to the IPC based Florida Building Code, Plumbing. We make the line safe first, then confirm with the city building division whether the permanent fix needs a permit.

After the repair: drying and documenting

Getting the pipe fixed is half the job. Wet drywall, insulation and flooring need to dry quickly, usually with fans and dehumidifiers, to avoid mold. Remove soaked materials that cannot dry, and keep receipts.

We write down what failed and where, which helps with claims and with any other work we recommend. If the pipe that failed was part of an aging run, we will point out where other sections could fail so you can plan. For widespread problems, repiping is the larger fix.
